## Directive 1: Replace the [AUTHOR INPUT NEEDED] placeholder under Eliminate with the sports NFT story

**What to write:** A brief first-person narrative (3-5 sentences) about the author's sports NFT collection. The story has two moves:

1. The author looked at a hobby he loved -- a sports NFT collection -- and realized it was consuming 2x more time than the joy warranted. He sold half the collection. Back-of-the-envelope math: 90% of the joy for 50% of the time.

2. After the narrative, teach the distinction between **partial elimination** and **full elimination**. Most people think of elimination as all-or-nothing -- bowing out of a committee you never should have joined (which is, of course, an excellent thing to do). But elimination also includes trimming. The NFT story is partial elimination of a gray item, not cancellation of an obligation. This is what makes it useful -- it expands the reader's sense of what "eliminate" can mean.

**Voice requirement:** The NFT collection is a hobby the author genuinely enjoys. The tone should be fond and self-aware, not apologetic. The author is trimming something he likes because the time math stopped working, not confessing to a vice. Think of the board games in Ch 1 -- same register of affection for a nerdy hobby.

**Kill:** Remove the `[AUTHOR INPUT NEEDED: ...]` placeholder entirely, including the bracketed example shape. Also remove the sentence "The commitment that never should have been made. The obligation that serves no one. The thing you've been doing out of habit or guilt or a vague sense that you 'should.'" -- this is generic self-help language that the NFT story renders unnecessary. The story shows what elimination looks like; the reader doesn't need the abstract list of what might qualify.

**Preserve:** Keep the closing paragraph about elimination being underused ("Elimination is the most underused option..."), but voice-pass it per Directive 5.

## Directive 2: Add the leaf-blowing story to Delegate

**What to write:** A brief first-person narrative (2-3 sentences) inserted BEFORE the existing teaching about embodied vs. unembodied work. The story:

The author realized yard work he felt neutral about -- blowing leaves -- was within his son's capabilities, and hired him. The key detail: "I love raking. I can take or leave leaf blowing." This line is peak labnotes voice -- protect it exactly or very close to exactly. The confession that raking is genuinely enjoyable (and yes, it sounds dorky) is the kind of self-aware specificity that earns the right to teach.

**Why this story matters for the teaching:** It sets up the delegate criteria naturally. A chore isn't a great candidate for delegation if you genuinely enjoy it, even if it's a chore. Leaf blowing qualified because (a) the author was neutral on it, (b) his son could do it competently, and (c) things wouldn't get worse for having delegated it. This is practical judgment, not a framework -- which is exactly how delegation decisions should read.

**Placement:** Open the Delegate section with the leaf-blowing story (1-2 sentences of scene, then the "I love raking" line), THEN transition to the existing teaching about embodied vs. unembodied work. The story earns the right to teach -- confess before teaching, per the voice rules.

**Kill:** Remove the opening sentence "Some tabs are real tasks that need doing -- just not by you." -- this is the generic self-help line flagged in the scorecard. The leaf-blowing story replaces it as the section opener.

## Directive 5: Voice-pass the following flagged lines

These lines were specifically identified in the v5 scorecard as failing the voice gate. Each one could appear in any self-help book about personal productivity. Rewrite so they could only appear in THIS book, written by THIS author.

**Line 1:**

> "The goal isn't to complete some elaborate project. The goal is resolution. And resolution can take several forms."

This is the Beat 4 opener (after the accountability email). It's depersonalized and reads as a keynote transition. Rewrite it through the author's experience -- what did the author learn about resolution from settling the credit card tab? The credit card wasn't a project. It was a ten-minute form. The insight is that resolution is often simpler than the dread suggests. Ground the line in that.

**Line 2:**

> "Some tabs are recurring irritations that could run without you."

This opens Automate and has the same generic-self-help problem. The supplements story is right there -- let the story open the section. Cut or fold this line into the narrative transition.

**Line 3:**

> "Pick the method that fits your tab. If you're unsure, start with eliminate. Most people skip that option because it feels final. That's exactly why it's powerful."

This is the closing paragraph of the four methods, before the "put the book down" line. "Most people skip that option" is third-person generalization. "That's exactly why it's powerful" is a motivational-speaker button. Rewrite through the author's lens: what did the author learn about choosing between methods? Why does eliminate get skipped? Anchor in first person.

**Line 4:**

> "Elimination is the most underused option. We're trained to solve problems, not dissolve them. The discomfort of saying 'I can't continue with this' is real, but it's a one-time cost in exchange for eliminating a recurring drain."

"We're trained to solve problems, not dissolve them" is an aphorism that reads as borrowed wisdom. "A one-time cost in exchange for eliminating a recurring drain" is analytical language that doesn't sound like the author. Rewrite so it sounds like the author reflecting on why he didn't sell the NFTs sooner -- or why elimination feels counterintuitive in general, grounded in his experience.
