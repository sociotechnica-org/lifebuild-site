# Voice Samples — Danvers Fleury

These are excerpts from the author's SocioTechnica labnotes. They represent the target voice for _Boss @ Work | Intern @ Life_. Study them before drafting.

---

## Sample 1: "Can AI Cure My Addiction to Overcommitting?" (WS005)

### Opening — Confession as setup

> During the pandemic, my work-life balance swung wide and to the right. Work travel vanished. My startup crashed and burned. Socializing meant playing D&D on Zoom with friends who lived less than 25 minutes away. I briefly took up home appliance repair as a hobby (???). Strange times indeed.
>
> As the busyness drained from my life, I experienced relief, then joy, then boredom. Then came the reckoning.
>
> The boredom forced an honest audit of how my time spent matched my stated values. Even before lockdown, my community involvement consisted of superficial, performative acts. I claimed health was my top priority while prioritizing wine and pizza. Most of my identity had wrapped itself around a startup that no longer existed, with no plan to backfill that void. When you're sitting alone in your home office for eighteen months, the truth becomes impossible to ignore.

**What to notice:** Escalating list with pattern break ("relief, then joy, then boredom. Then came the reckoning."). One-sentence paragraph for impact. Self-implication without self-pity. The pandemic detail is specific (D&D on Zoom, home appliance repair) not generic.

### Format-as-punchline — The professional/personal infrastructure gap

> **Process Infrastructure For My Favorite Client:**
>
> - System Level: Yearlong, multi-phase contracts with defined goals
> - Project Level: Sprint kickoffs and retrospectives
> - Task Level: Trello board for task tracking, daily standups
>
> **Process Infrastructure For Home Maintenance:**
>
> - My wife writes things on a whiteboard
> - We ignore them for months
> - Eventually they won't erase, even with spray

**What to notice:** The structure IS the joke. The professional side is formatted like a proper org chart. The personal side descends into chaos. No commentary needed — the contrast does the work.

### Executive function as structural, not moral

> Without AI, I'd never attempt more responsibility in my personal life — I have the executive function of a garden hose. But given the leaps and bounds that models have made in the last 60 days, and the anticipated quantum leaps to come in the coming weeks, it feels foolish not to fix this.

**What to notice:** "Executive function of a garden hose" is the author's signature move — treating a real limitation as a design constraint, not a character flaw. The sentence doesn't apologize. It states the constraint and moves to the engineering response.

### Gaming references as native vocabulary

> A game master to shrink my daily and weekly context window. Sim City starts with a city block. Civilization begins with one settler. I'm currently staring at a world on fire and want to turn off the computer.

**What to notice:** Not "it's like playing a video game." Just the reference, deployed as the natural frame. "I want to turn off the computer" works as both literal (overwhelm) and gaming metaphor simultaneously. No explanation needed.

## Sample 2: "Working My Life Into Burnout" (WS006)

### Specific numbers over generalizations

> The formula produced what I now call my Whoops! score, ranging from +10 to -10. Negative is bad. Really negative is really bad.
>
> - Homeowner: -10
> - Head of Household: -9
> - Four other categories: Also on fire
> - Fitness & Health: -2 (my star performer!)

**What to notice:** Register shift — "ranging from +10 to -10" is analytical, "Negative is bad. Really negative is really bad" is colloquial. The list uses specific scores, then breaks format with "Also on fire" and "(my star performer!)". The parenthetical asides carry the emotional commentary.

### Physical emotion without interpretation

> When it was over, I sagged, demoralized and dreading my next to-do.

**What to notice:** "I sagged" is physical. Then two emotional states named plainly. No interpretation ("this is what burnout feels like"), no generalization ("we all know that feeling"). Just the body and the feeling. Stop.

### Specificity as credibility

> Here's how hard:
>
> - 19 emails
> - 30 document uploads
> - 4 phone calls
> - 3-4 DocuSign attempts (depending on how you count)
> - 10 days off the calendar

**What to notice:** 19, not 20. 3-4, not "several." The parenthetical adds texture. These are counted, not estimated. The precision makes the absurdity real.

### Other people's words, deployed precisely

> At that point my partner Jess dropped the truth bomb: "We experience real switching costs between tasks, projects, and categories."

**What to notice:** The quote is set up as a "truth bomb" — the author was living the problem and someone else named it. This is a move the book should use: let other voices (Jess, colleagues, researchers) deliver key insights while the author provides the experiential context.

## Anti-patterns — The Manuscript Voice to Avoid

These are from the existing book chapters. They represent the voice the revision should move AWAY from:

> "Most people drown in bronze. They spend their weeks in maintenance mode, telling themselves they'll get to the important stuff once they catch up. They rarely catch up."

**Problem:** Third-person generalization. "Most people" when "I" would be more honest and more persuasive.

> "That's a taste of what sovereignty feels like. Not the grand version, not the final destination. One small piece reclaimed. One decision that went the way you chose, not the way your defaults chose for you."

**Problem:** Emotional interpretation from a retrospective distance. The author is narrating the feeling instead of letting the reader feel it. Also uses eliminated language ("sovereignty").

> "Psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi identified this distinction in his landmark book Flow. He separates pleasure from enjoyment — and the difference matters."

**Problem:** Academic citation style in prose. The author should use the finding, not introduce the researcher. "Your brain lies to you about rest. Csikszentmihalyi's research shows why: pleasure (what restores you) and enjoyment (what engages you) feel similar but do opposite things to your capacity."

> "These are the micro-skills of sovereignty. They don't require special tools or elaborate systems — just clarity about what's hurting, the courage to address it, and a willingness to do something different than before."

**Problem:** Actively argues against the book's thesis by framing the win as personal virtue (clarity, courage, willingness) rather than architectural change. Also uses eliminated language.
