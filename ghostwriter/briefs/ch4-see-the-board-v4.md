## VOICE DIRECTIVE -- READ THIS FIRST

This chapter failed voice audit three times. The root cause is now diagnosed and the system prompt is patched. Read this section before anything else.

**The diagnosis:** v1-v3 treated "teaching register" as permission to write depersonalized instructional prose. The result: Beats 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, and 7 read like a well-organized self-help briefing that could appear in any productivity book. Beat 4 is the only beat that consistently sounds like Danvers. That is because Beat 4 is a first-person narrative -- the one mode the ghostwriter was never confused about.

**The fix:** There is no "teaching register" that is separate from labnotes voice. The locked chapters prove this. Ch 2 teaches bronze/silver/gold through a specific night playing a strategy game and mold on the porch. Ch 1 teaches the infrastructure gap through the dishrag scene and the board games that never made it to Craigslist. Every framework in this book is taught through personal failure or a specific scene.

**The rule for this draft:** Every section -- including teaching sections -- must open with a first-person scene, confession, or specific personal observation before switching to second-person instruction. No exceptions. The confession earns the right to teach. If you have been in second-person "you" mode for more than 150 words, you have lost the voice. Return to "I."

**Moves that are now banned:**

- "Consider how much of your professional work is visible." (The "consider" pivot -- TED talk move, not labnotes)
- "Your projects have dashboards. Your team has a shared board." (Narrating the reader's professional life for them -- write your OWN experience and let the reader recognize the pattern)
- "Part III is where each micro-skill becomes a permanent capability." (Meta-commentary about book structure in prose -- the reader should never see the blueprint)
- "Most productivity advice treats visibility as binary." (Depersonalized instructional opening -- where is the author?)
- Sustained second-person teaching for more than one paragraph without first-person return

**The Voice Gate -- apply to every paragraph:**

1. Could this paragraph appear in a generic self-help book about personal productivity? If yes, rewrite.
2. Does this paragraph open with the author's experience or a depersonalized instruction? If instruction, add a first-person anchor before the teaching.
3. Has second-person teaching been sustained for more than 150 words without a first-person return? If yes, interrupt with a confession, scene, or specific observation.
4. Does any research finding read as "Scientists found..." or "In [year], [researcher] discovered..."? If yes, deploy the finding as argument. Name the researcher in a footnote.

Open with the author's own professional visibility infrastructure -- what THEIR dashboards looked like, what THEIR workflow visibility was. Every agent, every task, every bottleneck -- visible. I could see what was moving, what was stuck, what was waiting for a human decision. ### BEAT 2: The Spectrum of Visibility (800-1,000 words, REWRITE FROM SCRATCH with carried-forward sentences)

**Voice register:** Teaching voice anchored in first person. Research-backed but confessional. Dense and rewarding. The research is ammunition, not curriculum.

**How to open in labnotes register:** DO NOT open with "Most productivity advice treats visibility as binary." That is depersonalized instructional prose -- no author present. Open with the author's own experience of the spectrum. Where did THEY start? "In your head" is where. Open with what that felt like -- the lying-awake-at-night paragraph is the strongest empathy moment in this beat. Lead with it. The author's personal experience of holding everything in working memory, THEN the Cowan finding that explains why.

**The spectrum structure (four subsections):**

**Holding Everything in Your Head:**

- OPEN with the author's experience. "This is where I started and where I stayed for years" is good -- it is already first person. Build from there.
- Working memory limit of four items (Cowan). Deploy as argument after the felt experience. Footnote the study.
- The lying-awake-at-night paragraph -- vivid, the reader will recognize themselves.
- ADD the punch line: "Four. If you have a dentist appointment, a work deadline, an overdue email, and a grocery list, you are at capacity. Everything else is either gone or costing you."

**Writing Things Down:**

- OPEN with the author's experience of lists. Not "Cognitive scientists call it cognitive offloading." The author's experience of writing things down and the relief AND the limitation. Then the concept, credited in footnote.
- The 13ms image processing stat: deploy as argument, not reported finding. "Your brain identifies images in 13 milliseconds -- faster than a single eye blink" is correct.
- KEEP: "Lists flatten what the mind naturally organizes spatially." and "Your brain wants to put things in places. Lists refuse to let it."
- Return to first person before the section ends. "I had lists. Plenty of lists. I had a to-do app with forty-seven items on it and no instinct for where to start." (Or similar -- the author's specific experience.)

**Spatial Organization:**

- Place cells, Nobel Prize -- deploy as argument. No chronological reporting.
- KEEP: "Your brain won a Nobel Prize's worth of spatial architecture, and you've been feeding it bullet points."
- Maguire taxi driver study: one sentence. Footnote.
- Kanban board example: two to three sentences.
- KEEP: "But if you have ever stared at a kanban board and still felt like you could not see your life..."
- Anchor in first person. "I tried kanban boards. I tried Notion. I could see the columns, but I couldn't see my life." (Or similar.)

**Navigable Environments:**

- Method of loci: two to three sentences. Dresler study finding preserved.
- VR study: one sentence.
- Video games: three sentences maximum.
- CLOSE: One sentence summarizing the spectrum. Anchor in first person: "I'd been stuck at the list level for years. I didn't know the spectrum existed."

**Kill list for this beat:** Extended video games section. Extended method of loci history. "Embodiment advantage" paragraph. Any study getting its own full paragraph. "Most people" generalizations. Any framing that reads like a literature review. Any research finding introduced with "Scientists found..." or "In [year], [researcher] discovered..." Any section that opens with depersonalized instruction rather than the author's experience.

## Warnings and Traps

1. **The depersonalized teaching trap.** This is THE trap that killed v1-v3. Every time you write a paragraph that could appear in any self-help book, stop and add the author. "Working memory holds roughly four items at once" is a finding. "I was holding forty-seven items in a brain that holds four" is the book's voice.

2. **The neuroscience trap.** Compress and weaponize. Do not give studies dignified academic treatment.

3. **The Beat 4 Marcus residue trap.** The scene must feel like a real memory.

4. **The teaching-without-anchoring trap.** Beats 2 and 3 are the longest teaching stretch. First-person anchoring every 150 words maximum.

5. **The Balance Gap ghost.** Three functions (overwhelm, prevention, imbalance) still need to land but through the audit vocabulary and promotion metaphor.

6. **The Zhang & Norman placement trap.** The quote stays in Beat 3. Do not move it.

7. **The "Consider" trap.** Banned. The author shows; the reader considers without being told to.
